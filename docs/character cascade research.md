### 5. Geometric‑phase sign flip under transport

**(a) Established name and subfield**  
**Berry phase / Hannay angle** associated with encircling (or passing through the vicinity of) a **degeneracy point (diabolical point or Dirac monopole)** in parameter space. In your classical dissipative system the correct classical analogue is the **Hannay angle**; in the underlying linear non‑Hermitian description it is the **Berry phase of the eigenmode**. The sign flip of the order parameter (handedness reversal) is the signature of a **π geometric phase** acquired when the path encloses a solid angle of \(2\pi\) (or, equivalently, when the path crosses a degeneracy locus).

**(b) Key canonical references**  
- M. V. Berry, “Quantal phase factors accompanying adiabatic changes,” *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* **392**, 45 (1984).  
- J. Hannay, “Angle variable holonomy in adiabatic excursion of classical integrable Hamiltonians,” *J. Phys. A* **18**, 221 (1985).  
- A. K. Pati, “Adiabatic Berry Phase and Hannay Angle for Open Paths,” *Annals of Physics* (1998) — extends the formula to non‑cyclic transport.  
- R. Bamler, “Phase‑Space Berry Phases in Chiral Magnets” (thesis) — gives the solid‑angle formula \(\gamma = -m\Omega\) and discusses sign flips from parallel transport on \(S^{2}\).

**(c) Exact formula / scaling law**  
For a spin‑\(m\) (or \(m\)-fold degenerate) system transported around a loop \(C\) on the parameter sphere \(S^{2}\), the Berry phase is  
\[
\boxed{\gamma(C) = -m\,\Omega(C)},
\]  
where \(\Omega(C)\) is the **solid angle** subtended by \(C\) at the degeneracy (monopole). A **sign flip** of the wavefunction or classical mode corresponds to \(\gamma = \pi\) (mod \(2\pi\)), i.e.,  
\[
\Omega = \frac{\pi}{m}.
\]  
For a classical director (effectively \(m=1/2\) or \(m=1\) depending on whether you track the mode or the envelope), a solid angle of \(2\pi\) gives a sign flip.

For an **open path** that goes from the north pole to polar angle \(\theta\) and back along a meridian, the solid angle enclosed is  
\[
\Omega(\theta) = 2\pi(1-\cos\theta).
\]  
Setting \(\Omega = \pi\) (for a spin‑1/2‑like mode) yields \(\cos\theta = 1/2\), i.e., \(\theta = 60^{\circ}\). Your measured critical tilt of **~39°** suggests either:
- a different path geometry (not a simple lune), or
- an effective “charge” (monopole strength) different from \(1/2\), or
- that the reversal is not a full Berry phase but rather a **passage through a node** (a degeneracy in the *real* spectrum, i.e., an EP) where the eigenmode swaps identity.

**(d) Standard dimensionless number**  
The solid angle \(\Omega\) is the natural dimensionless quantity. The “monopole charge” \(m\) is quantized by the degeneracy structure. For a generic two‑fold degeneracy in a three‑parameter space, \(m=\pm 1/2\).

**(e) Honest flag**  
The **solid‑angle law is absolutely canonical**. However, whether your specific **~39°** reversal angle maps cleanly onto a universal solid‑angle formula is **genuinely open** without knowing the exact path and the monopole location in your full 3‑unit parameter space. If the path is not a geodesic lune, or if the degeneracy is not at the center of the sphere, the critical angle will be model‑dependent. The sign flip itself is diagnostic of a Berry/Hannay phase; the numerical value 39° is a derived parameter of your model, not a universal constant.
