## Headline

**The mint-vs-redistribute question has an answer: a qualified yes.** Coupling *can* mint
protected structure — but only when it changes a topological/algebraic/graph-theoretic
invariant of the composite; when it merely shifts eigenvalues it redistributes. The
cleanest established case of minting is **signed-graph frustration**. The EP/oscillation
route generically gives *unprotected* structure. This forces a **re-route of Family C**, not
a promotion.

## The Family C re-route

Family C as written — "an EP crossing creates a *protected* circulation bit" — **does not
survive.** Generic EP-created structure is unprotected, and intrinsic finite-dim protection
is `[Open]`. It does not die either: it **re-routes.** Protection comes from **Family B3
(signed-graph frustration, `[Established]` minted + protected)**, not the EP. Restated:
*coupling-created graph frustration forces a complex pair and protects it; the EP is the
spectral onset, the frustration is the protection.* This is the framework's own iff-chain
(`𝒜≠0 ⟺ complex pair ⟺ triad`) read in the correct direction — protection inherited from
the gauge-irremovable triad (model a's own resolution: "circulation inherits protection from
the invariant rather than from the existence of a complex pair").

## Where the contribution now lives

The reports confirm the generative-metaphor thesis cleanly: **every node is imported**
(signed-graph minting, EP machinery, strong-symmetry DFS, Schnakenberg currents — all
`[Established]`), and the **edge is genuinely undrawn** — model a's disagreement list and
model c's Q3 both state that *no unified theorem* connects graph-frustration ⟺ spectral
complex-pair ⟺ thermodynamic `𝒜`. That binding is MPA's residual. And the central bet is now
precisely located on an `[Open]` field question: **is the chirality of a NESS circulation
protected in a generic finite-dimensional dissipative system?**

## Trap avoided

The union-graph frustration test is **trivial** — it re-derives textbook Harary balance,
the same circularity as the collapse demos. The non-trivial, contribution-grade test is the
**chirality-protection** test: couple a frustrated-union system, check whether the minted
current's chirality survives *all* continuous deformations (dies only on rewiring).
